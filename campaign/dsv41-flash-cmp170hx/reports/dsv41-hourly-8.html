2.Why c1 can't easily go higher (measured, not assumed)

  • Not power-bound: under a c1 load the GPUs draw ~90–100 W of the 250 W limit at 1470 MHz (max 1695). The ceiling is 300 W but there is no thermal/power pressure to relieve — c1 is memory/latency-bound.
  • DSpark works: c1 acceptance is 93.6% (len 4.68) on predictable content and ~35–45% on open prose; it is what lifts c1 well above the no-spec rate.
  • MoE backend: `moe_backend: triton` fails engine init on this MXFP4/SM80 path — Marlin is the only working MXFP4 MoE backend.
  • Width is fixed: the 475 GB checkpoint cannot fit one TP4 single stage, so TP8 is the minimum single-stage width; TP4×PP1/TP2×PP1 are impossible.
  • Draft count is fixed: the checkpoint's dspark_block_size=5 pins DSpark k=5.
Conclusion: c1 headroom past ~98 tok/s lives in the Marlin MXFP4 MoE GEMV and the sm_80 sparse-MLA decode kernel — kernel work, not configuration.

3.Tradeoff and next

  • TP8×PP1 costs 512k aggregate (c64 113 vs 130 at TP4×PP2). Both configs are kept one command apart.
  • Next c1 candidates: fused MoE GEMV for batch-1, or the upstream sparse-MLA decode work (HiSparse #53781 lineage).
